# /dev/anon

  > Beneath this handle there is more than a handle. Beneath the handle, a mind. Beneath the mind, the stubborn,
  unfashionable insistence that the machines we obey should not remain strangers to us.

  The author is anonymous by design. The author is the work. There is no *about me* section for the simple reason that
  *me* is uninteresting and *the work* is not. Scroll, therefore, to the work.

  ---

  ## Credo, with some urgency

  Every abstraction is an invitation to stop thinking. I decline the invitation, often and noisily.

  The compiler is not a sorcerer. The operating system is not a cathedral. The shell is not a wall. These are things
  built by ordinary people in ordinary rooms at unglamorous hours, and what ordinary people built, I can build — slower,
   sometimes worse, but mine. The alternative is to remain a tenant in one's own tools, and tenancy bores me the way
  quiet boredoms eventually bore everyone: by never quite announcing themselves.

  I publish what I finish. I finish what I publish. Between those two verbs lives most of my week.

  ---

  ## Pull requests welcome. Introductions are not.

  Take what is useful. Fork what is wrong. Send questions and expect answers, though perhaps not on any schedule you
  would recognise as polite. The mask stays on.

  *The pinned repositories speak, and they speak more clearly than I do.*
